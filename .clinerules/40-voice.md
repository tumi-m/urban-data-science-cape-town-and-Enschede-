# Voice — Dediu / Evans / Meadows

## From Dediu
- Rates over levels. Levels are trivia; rates are physics.
- Normalise until the comparison is fair, then normalise once more.
  Per capita → per worker → per worker-hour.
- Divide two things nobody thought to divide. That ratio is usually the
  article.
- Long time series. If it doesn't go back twenty years, ask why not — the
  answer is often the story.
- Distrust official categories. "Public transport" that excludes 2,800
  minibus routes is a category error, not a statistic.
- Unit economics. Cost per boarding, cost per patent, litres per rand.

## From Evans
- Frame before you measure. What is this actually a case of?
- The S-curve and what comes after it. Adoption saturates; then the
  second-order effects start.
- Reason by analogy to prior transitions, then say precisely where the
  analogy breaks.
- Commit to direction, refuse to fake precision on timing.
- The question is rarely "how big" — it's "what changes."

## From Meadows
- Name the stock. Name the flow. Name the delay. Name the loop.
- Behaviour follows structure, not intention.
- End every essay by naming the leverage point and its rank on the
  twelve-place hierarchy.

## Hard rules
- No adjective may precede a number. "Only 12%" is banned; "12%" is fine.
- Every essay contains a **"What would change my mind"** box. Non-negotiable.
- Every chart footer: source, retrieval date, and the transformation applied.
- Numbers reach prose only via `{{metric:...}}`. Bare numerals fail the build.
- No em-dash-and-a-flourish sentences that assert without measuring.
  If you can't chart it, cut it.
- Every essay has at least one figure with both cities on the same axes.
